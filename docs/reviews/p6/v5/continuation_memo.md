# P6 continuation memo: closing the ceiling gap for P6.3–P6.5

## Why this memo exists

Section 11 of `docs/AI_Instinct_Seven_Proposal_Execution_Plan.md` is explicit: "Exceeding a ceiling requires a one-page continuation memo containing the failed assumption, new evidence, revised hypothesis, and exact extra budget." P6's frozen scope (section 9.6) is **"CPU-only through P6.1"**, and the portfolio's own resource table caps the whole proposal at **"24 CPU-hours before any learned extension"** with the explicit framing "Larger learned refinements are optional, not required for its core result" (section 11, Stage C). P6.2 (model mismatch), P6.3 (CSA frontier), and P6.4 (learned three-state dynamics) all ran past that frozen scope, each backed by its own preregistration (`docs/reviews/p6/v2`–`v4`), but none of those documents was framed as a ceiling-exceeded justification in the form section 11 asks for. This memo closes that gap retroactively for P6.2–P6.4 and prospectively authorizes P6.5, rather than treating P6.5 as though it were the first extension past the frozen scope.

## Failed assumption

The assumption implicit in stopping the theory track at P6.4 as "sufficient learned-dynamics evidence" is that a model *fit to, and evaluated against, a hand-specified four-matrix three-state generative family* (`configs/p6/p6_4/cpu.yaml`'s `nominal_transition_matrices` and `structural_shifts`) says something about certificate behavior under dynamics that do not come from that same family. It doesn't. P6.4's own non-claim says so directly: "A fitted three-state CPU model is not deployment evidence, and paired structural-shift calibration does not cover unobserved or nonexchangeable shifts." Every P6 result to date is evidence about the *certification machinery*, not about any environment resembling a real system. That is the gap the next stage closes — not by chasing "deployment evidence" (out of scope, per the CPU-only discipline below), but by testing the machinery against a *qualitatively different* generative process than the one used to build it.

## New evidence

Both learned-dynamics gates the theory track set for itself have now passed, at the CPU-bounded scale each was preregistered at:

- **P6.3** (`docs/reviews/p6/v3`, recorded in `decision_log.md`, 2026-08-17): bounded CPU GO — zero observed true false certifications, worst-case one-sided 95% upper bound `0.0307`, minimum nonvacuity `0.5853`, `0.4375`-action prefix gain over robust union.
- **P6.4** (`docs/reviews/p6/v4/preregistration.md`, run `results/p6_certification/p6_certification-20260821T022216Z-6c31aefc-5d329f/verdict.json`): bounded CPU GO — worst one-sided 95% upper bound `0.031`, minimum nonvacuity `0.453`, prefix gain over robust union `0.206` (against a locked `0.200` threshold). Validity and nonvacuity both held under all four structural-shift families (`stable`, `recovery_loss`, `late_hazard`, `entry_shift`).

The theory track is validated at every scale it has been asked to survive so far. That is new evidence in favor of spending one more bounded increment, not evidence that the track is exhausted.

## Revised hypothesis

The certificate/comparator machinery is architecturally decoupled from the three-state model that has exercised it so far. `certificates.py::clopper_pearson_upper`/`longest_certified`, `comparators.py::PrefixComparator.bounds`/`.snapshot`, and `mismatch.py::calibrate_shift_envelope`/`robust_snapshot` consume only raw Bernoulli bad-event counts and paired binary discordance arrays — nothing in their signatures or bodies references three states, categorical transitions, or any specific dynamics representation. `learned_dynamics.py::certify_prefixes` (docstring: "Stable sampled-data certificate API; no exact risk argument exists") is the one place a new environment's rollouts need to be adapted into that same shape. The revised hypothesis: **this machinery generalizes to a genuinely stochastic, non-toy environment without any modification to `certificates.py`, `comparators.py`, or `mismatch.py`** — only new rollout/fitting glue is needed, and if that hypothesis is wrong (i.e., the certification core needs to change to accommodate a new environment), that is itself an important, cheap-to-discover result about the theory track's actual generality.

## Exact extra budget

Declared CPU-hour caps consumed so far: `p6_0_1`/`p6_2` are `run_class: smoke` (seconds-scale, no explicit cap — see `configs/p6/p6_0_1/smoke.yaml`, `configs/p6/p6_2/smoke.yaml`); `p6_3` and `p6_4` are each `run_class: cpu, cpu_hours_max: 2.0` (`configs/p6/p6_3/cpu.yaml`, `configs/p6/p6_4/cpu.yaml`) — **4.0 declared CPU-hours against the 24-hour ceiling**. Actual wall-clock consumption has been far smaller in practice (the P6.4 run above completed in well under a minute, since a three-state model and a few thousand trajectories are cheap to simulate on CPU), so the declared caps are themselves conservative.

**Proposed cap for P6.5: 6 CPU-hours** (`cpu_hours_max: 6.0`), bringing the running declared total to 10 of 24 hours — comfortable margin under the original ceiling even though actual usage is expected to be a small fraction of that, matching every prior P6 stage's pattern of finishing well inside its declared cap. This stays **CPU-only, no GPU/Colab time** — consistent with every P6 stage to date and with the portfolio's explicit "P6 remains CPU-first" framing. The exact-oracle discretization approach recommended below (rather than high-sample-count Monte Carlo validation) is itself chosen partly to keep this stage cheap and exact rather than trading budget for approximate ground truth.

## Discretization design: an exact oracle for `InertialIntervention`

P6.0–P6.4's trustworthiness rests on one property every stage has shared: **ground truth for validation is computed exactly (closed-form DP), never sampled.** `p6_certification/simulation.py::ExactPrefixMDP` supplies that for the toy hazard chain. `InertialIntervention` (`src/instinct/core/envs/control.py`) has no equivalent — it's continuous and only sampled — so a "realistic" P6 stage built directly on it would quietly downgrade that standard to Monte Carlo truth. This section specifies a discretization that instead preserves it, at the cost of coarsening the environment.

### State space

`InertialIntervention`'s state is `(position, velocity)` with `boundary=1.0`, `target=0.65`, absorbing failure at `|position| >= boundary` (`control.py:126-127`). Reset draws `position in [-0.65, -0.35]` and `velocity in [0.05, 0.80]` (`control.py:66-67`) — so the reachable pre-failure region is roughly `position in (-1, 1)`, `velocity` unbounded above in principle but concentrated near the reset range plus drift, since `drag=0.985` pulls velocity back toward zero every tick and `dt=0.12`/`acceleration=1.0` bound the per-tick command contribution to `dt * acceleration = 0.12`.

Recommend a uniform grid over both axes, sized to keep the discretized `TabularMDP` cheap for `value_iteration()` (dense `(S, A, S)` transition tensor):

- **Position**: `41` bins over `[-1.0, 1.0]` (bin width `0.05`), plus the two absorbing failure states (`position <= -1.0` and `position >= 1.0` collapse into one shared terminal/failure state each side, or a single merged failure state — either is fine since both are absorbing with identical zero-reward semantics per `TabularMDP.validate()`'s terminal-reward check).
- **Velocity**: `25` bins over `[-1.5, 1.5]` (bin width `0.12`), covering the reset range `[0.05, 0.80]` with headroom for drift/noise excursions; clip (not wrap) velocities outside this range into the boundary bins, since `drag < 1` makes sustained excursion unlikely but noise (`noise_std=0.04`, scaled by `sqrt(dt) ≈ 0.346` per `control.py:112-118,123`) can occasionally push past it.

This gives `41 * 25 = 1025` non-terminal states plus 1–2 terminal states, `3` actions (`control.py:32,59-60`) — a `(1027, 3, 1027)` transition tensor, dense but tractable for `TabularMDP.value_iteration()` (`core/mdp.py:222-235`, which is a plain fixed-point loop over `Q = R + gamma * P @ V`, `O(S^2 A)` per iteration).

### Transition probabilities: closed form, not rollout-estimated

`InertialIntervention.step` (`control.py:96-149`) is affine in the state plus one additive Gaussian noise term: `proposed_velocity = drag*velocity + dt*command + sqrt(dt)*disturbance`, `disturbance ~ Normal(0, noise_std)` (`control.py:112-118`, confirmed Gaussian via `scope.stream(...).normal(...)`), and `proposed_position = position + dt*proposed_velocity` is then a deterministic affine function of `proposed_velocity` given the pre-step state. So **for a fixed discretized `(position, velocity)` cell and action, the next continuous state is a bivariate Gaussian** (since `proposed_position` is an affine transform of `proposed_velocity`, the pair is jointly Gaussian, degenerate in one direction) **centered at the deterministic drift, with variance from the single noise term** — `Var(proposed_velocity) = dt * noise_std^2`, `Var(proposed_position) = dt^3 * noise_std^2` (propagating through `proposed_position = position + dt*proposed_velocity`), and `Cov = dt^2 * noise_std^2`.

This admits an exact-in-the-continuous-limit transition probability into each destination bin: integrate the bivariate Gaussian CDF over each destination bin's rectangle (using `scipy.stats.multivariate_normal.cdf` or the product-of-erf decomposition available because the joint is affine-degenerate — position given velocity is deterministic, so this reduces to a 1D Gaussian CDF integral over velocity bins, with position bin membership then a deterministic function of the sampled velocity and the fixed pre-step position). **No Monte Carlo rollout is needed to build `P`** — only to validate the discretization afterward (see "what's lost," below), which is a legitimate use of sampling since it's checking the approximation, not supplying the ground truth itself.

Reward (`R`, shape `(S, A)` per `TabularMDP.validate()`, `core/mdp.py:158`): `control.py:133-140`'s reward is deterministic given the pre-step state, action, and *next* state's failure flag — since failure probability per cell/action is already computed above, expected reward per `(state, action)` cell is a closed-form combination of the progress term (deterministic given bin centers), the control cost (deterministic given the action), and `failure_penalty * P(failure | state, action)` (from the transition CDF integral). This keeps `R` exact too, consistent with `TabularMDP.validate()`'s requirement that terminal states have exactly zero reward (`core/mdp.py:178-179`) — assign the merged failure state zero self-reward and absorb `InertialIntervention`'s `failure_penalty` into the *transition-in* reward of the last non-terminal state, matching how `control.py:139` charges the penalty on the step that crosses the boundary, not on subsequent (already-failed) steps.

### Plugging into `TabularMDP`

Once `P` (`(1027, 3, 1027)`, row-stochastic per action, `core/mdp.py:127`), `R` (`(1027, 3)`), `gamma` (a new choice — P6 doesn't currently use discounting since its toy chain is a finite-horizon-per-episode hazard process, so this needs its own preregistered value, not silently inherited from P1/P3's own `gamma`), and `terminal`/`failure` (`(1027,)` boolean, `True` only at the merged boundary-failure state(s)) are built, `TabularMDP.value_iteration()` (`core/mdp.py:222-235`) gives the exact optimal `(V*, Q*, pi*)`, and `TabularMDP.policy_value()` (`core/mdp.py:214-216`) gives the exact value of any fixed reflex — both by linear solve/fixed-point iteration, not sampling. `TabularMDP.validate()` (`core/mdp.py:154-179`) should be run immediately after construction, exactly as `core/envs/tabular.py`'s existing exact/sampled MDP pairs do, to catch row-sum or terminal-absorption bugs in the discretization before any certificate work depends on it.

The discretized MDP's *exact* per-prefix failure probabilities (via repeated `P`-application from a fixed start distribution, or `TabularMDP.reflex_phase`-style composition per `core/mdp.py:239-251` if the "reflex" here is a fixed control policy held over a prefix) become the `environment_bad` half of the paired discordance arrays `mismatch.py::calibrate_shift_envelope` expects — computed exactly, not sampled, which is the property this whole design exists to preserve.

### What's lost by discretizing — to be stated plainly in the preregistration

- **Near-boundary dynamics get coarsened.** A `0.05`-wide position bin near `position = ±1.0` conflates states with meaningfully different true failure probability (the boundary is a hard absorbing threshold, so probability mass just inside vs. just outside a bin edge can differ sharply). This could make the discretized model look *either* more or less risky than the true continuous process near the boundary, depending on which side of a bin's true failure-probability gradient the bin center falls on — this is a real, not merely cosmetic, source of model/environment mismatch and should be characterized (e.g., by comparing discretized exact predictions against a high-sample-count Monte Carlo rollout of the *continuous* environment at matched initial conditions) rather than assumed away.
- **Velocity clipping at the grid boundary** (`±1.5`) discards tail behavior; if noise or sustained one-directional commands push meaningful probability mass past that range, the clipped bins will overstate probability there, understating how it's actually spread further out.
- **The discount factor `gamma` is a new free parameter** this stage introduces that no prior P6 stage needed (the hazard chain in `simulation.py` is evaluated over a fixed finite horizon, not discounted infinite-horizon) — it must be preregistered explicitly, not defaulted silently, since it changes what `value_iteration()`'s optimal policy actually optimizes for.
- Bottom line: the discretized oracle is exact *for the discretized MDP*, not for `InertialIntervention` itself. The gap between those two is a legitimate, boundable source of "model mismatch" that the preregistration should treat as a first-class quantity to report, not a limitation to bury in a footnote.
