# P6.3 adversarial scale audit

## Scope and disposition

This is an independent pre-scale review of the frozen P6.3 CPU frontier. It
does not revise the P6.3 result or implementation. The zero-failure result is
useful evidence inside the declared IID nested-Bernoulli simulation, but it is
not sufficient for a learned-dynamics scale claim.

**Disposition: BLOCKER on any scale, deployment, or general-CSA claim.** The
learned-dynamics integration may proceed only as a fresh experiment after the
mandatory controls below are preregistered. P6.3 remains a bounded E2
mechanism result.

Severity meanings:

- **BLOCKER:** can create an apparent valid, nonvacuous, or fast result while
  the claimed learned-dynamics property is absent.
- **MAJOR:** does not necessarily invalidate the bounded result, but can reverse
  the utility or generalization conclusion.
- **MINOR:** reporting or robustness debt that must be repaired before a scale
  package is complete.

## Findings

### B1 — BLOCKER: the 8,192-exit advantage can be generated by a phantom exit schedule

`run_frontier_benchmark` computes certificates only at the five configured
sample sizes. Nevertheless, `shift_robust_union` pays a Bonferroni penalty for
8,192 exits, while the CSA snapshot receives its Ville guarantee without that
penalty. No policy actually checks 8,192 exits or adaptively stops at them. The
pilot record explicitly says dense declared exits created the CSA advantage.

This is finite-grid gaming: increasing an unused `declared_looks` value weakens
the comparator without adding CSA runtime or new stopping decisions. It also
violates the intended comparison to the strongest robust union for the exits
actually available to the agent.

Mandatory control: the learned integration must execute the real stopping
loop at every declared exit, record exits reached and checked per decision,
and compare against (i) a union over exactly those realized opportunities,
(ii) the best preregistered coarsened exit schedule, and (iii) an
anytime-valid non-CSA comparator. Sweep exit-grid refinements and coarsenings
without retuning thresholds. A gain that exists only when the baseline is
charged for unused exits is a STOP.

### B2 — BLOCKER: the paired shift envelope uses a privileged coupling unavailable in general learned dynamics

Every calibration repetition draws one uniform matrix and thresholds it under
both the learned MDP and true MDP. This is a maximal common-random-number
coupling. It minimizes positive discordance when true risk exceeds model risk.
For example, with model risk `0.10` and true risk `0.19`, this coupling has
positive discordance `0.09`; independent pairing has discordance
`0.19 * 0.90 = 0.171`. The smaller envelope materially increases certifiable
prefix length.

The inequality behind the envelope is valid for any genuine coupling, but a
real system usually cannot replay the environment's latent transition noise
inside its learned model. Matching an initial state or seed does not establish
counterfactual noise alignment after model and environment states diverge.

Mandatory control: specify the operational pairing mechanism before data are
seen. Run an independent-pair baseline, a same-initial-state/different-noise
baseline, and any claimed common-noise coupling with an auditable structural
justification. Report envelope and utility sensitivity to coupling. The scale
claim must use the weakest coupling actually available at deployment, not the
most favorable simulator coupling.

### B3 — BLOCKER: calibration reuse is valid only under a much narrower freeze than a learned integration normally has

P6.3 recalibrates within each known synthetic shift family and conditions on
an independent fixed calibration sample. It does not establish validity when
one envelope is reused across changed policies, model checkpoints, prefix
generators, tasks, subgroups, or time periods. It also does not exercise the
original repeated-episode confidence/risk spending target.

Concrete failure: calibrate where model/true risks are `0.10/0.11`, then reuse
the envelope after environment risk moves to `0.30` while model risk remains
`0.10`. The model e-process becomes increasingly confident and certifies an
unsafe prefix; more rollouts make the wrong certificate more certain. The
fixed envelope offers no protection because paired exchangeability has ended.

Mandatory control: freeze the learned model, rollout policy, action-prefix
family, loss, threshold, and deployment population for each calibration
epoch. Give envelopes explicit scope and expiry metadata. Any model update,
policy update, family expansion, or detected drift triggers fresh calibration
or a separately proved time-uniform shift process. Maintain an episode-level
`alpha_t` and `delta_t` ledger and test repeated adaptive decisions; a fresh
`0.05` confidence event per episode is not an episode-spending policy.

### B4 — BLOCKER: paired exchangeability and IID sampling are asserted, not stress-tested

The proof needs IID model-rollout losses and calibration/evaluation pairs from
the same discordance law. Learned systems commonly violate both through batched
correlated rollouts, replayed initial states, temporal drift, off-policy logs,
adaptive hard-example selection, and state divergence under the proposed
prefix.

Counterexample: let all 8,192 apparent rollout outcomes share one latent
Bernoulli variable with unsafe marginal risk `0.25`. With probability `0.75`
all counts are zero and the IID likelihood ratio certifies the unsafe prefix.
The effective sample size is one, not 8,192.

Mandatory control: define the independent statistical unit and cluster ID;
report unique environments, episodes, initial states, and stochastic roots.
Add block/cluster-resampled bounds, effective-sample-size diagnostics, temporal
holdouts, adversarial hard-state selection, and off-policy cells. Calibration,
certification rollouts, and execution evaluation must have disjoint unit
registries. Any unexplained dependence or calibration/evaluation discordance
shift blocks the validity claim.

### B5 — BLOCKER: current nonvacuity does not establish useful action execution

The implementation defines
`nonvacuity_rate = selected_total / (repetitions * n_prefixes)`. This is mean
normalized prefix length, not a nonabstention probability. The gate merely
requires it to be positive. If 95 of 96 repetitions abstain and one selects
all 16 actions, the criterion is positive (`1/96`) and passes. It can also pass
by repeatedly certifying a useless or no-op first action.

P6.3 reports no task return, success, action correctness, or oracle-relative
safe-prefix efficiency. Thus it does not test frozen section 9.5's requirement
to execute materially more useful actions at similar return. It also measures
false certification of a risk threshold, not repeated episode violation
probability.

Mandatory control: separately report nonabstention rate with a confidence
interval, median and lower-decile selected prefix, abstention by subgroup,
fraction of the oracle-safe prefix recovered, bound slack, realized episode
violations, task return/success, and rollback cost. Lock minimum criteria for
both execution frequency and task utility. Positive mean normalized length is
descriptive only and cannot be a verdict gate.

### B6 — BLOCKER: the 87x number is an implementation microbenchmark, not latency scaling

Certificate timing starts after cumulative bad counts and the shift envelope
already exist. It excludes rollout generation, learned-model inference,
per-exit updates, environment pairing, device synchronization, action dispatch,
and rollback. It evaluates five snapshots rather than 8,192 online checks.
The union path repeatedly calls an exact beta quantile; CSA uses elementary
logs. The result therefore largely compares SciPy special-function overhead
with arithmetic, not two optimized end-to-end systems.

Even within the synthetic measurement, adding the reported shared calibration
cost changes the largest-scale ratio from `87.45x` certificate-only to roughly
`(476.90 + 742.87) / (5.45 + 742.87) = 1.63x`, before rollout cost is counted.
The existing result correctly disclaims an end-to-end speedup; any later scale
claim must preserve that boundary.

Mandatory control: benchmark matched optimized implementations with warmup,
randomized method order, repeated processes, and p50/p95/p99 intervals. Include
all critical-path work: calibration amortization and refresh, rollout/model
inference, statistic updates at every real exit, selection, synchronization,
transfer, and action dispatch. Report throughput, memory, and device utilization
as well as latency. Compare actual exit schedules and include a vectorized or
lookup-optimized union baseline.

### M1 — MAJOR: the fixed CSA alternative has a permanent near-threshold power hole

For adjusted null `q = 0.18`, fixed alternative `theta = 0.16`, and genuinely
safe risk `p = 0.175`, the expected log likelihood-ratio increment is

`p log(theta/q) + (1-p) log((1-theta)/(1-q)) < 0`.

The e-value therefore tends toward zero rather than infinity even though
`p < q`. More data will not certify this safe prefix. A family concentrated
near `theta` makes the fixed bet look powerful; a family concentrated just
below `q` makes it asymptotically vacuous. Pilot selection of the `0.02` margin
does not resolve this failure.

Mandatory control: include dense near-boundary cells on both sides of every
adjusted threshold, multiple risk budgets, and bet margins fixed before fresh
families. Compare a valid mixture or predictable adaptive-betting e-process
that has power across the composite alternative. Report worst-family power,
not only average selected prefix.

### M2 — MAJOR: prefix-grid and threshold choices can reverse the gain

The experiment uses one 16-prefix grid, one risk threshold (`0.20`), uniform
Bonferroni prefix weights, and synthetic risks deliberately concentrated near
that threshold. Adding duplicate/irrelevant prefixes weakens both finite-family
methods; removing difficult prefixes strengthens them. Coarsening the exit
grid strongly benefits the union. Prefix length in “actions” also assumes each
candidate has equal utility and cost.

Mandatory control: preregister family construction independently of outcome
data; sweep risk thresholds, prefix-grid coarsening/refinement, irrelevant
candidate insertion, and nonuniform weights. Use semantic action cost/utility
in addition to index length. Family growth must be accounted for statistically;
post-calibration candidate generation is prohibited without new multiplicity
control or fresh splitting.

### M3 — MAJOR: four hand-authored shift shapes are not a family-generalization test

The stable, diffuse, late-cliff, and early-shift arrays are useful mechanism
controls, but calibration and evaluation occur inside each known family. A new
seed is not a new shift family. The average `0.4375`-action gain can hide the
late-cliff gain of only `0.09375`, and no paired confidence interval for gain is
used in the verdict.

Uncovered cases include rare catastrophic subgroups, state-conditional shift,
mixtures whose weights change after calibration, middle-prefix cliffs,
continuous/non-Bernoulli losses, off-policy prefixes, and task families where
failure is not nested.

Mandatory control: reserve entire learned tasks, dynamics models, shift
mechanisms, and demographic/state subgroups as fresh evaluation families.
Report family-adjusted validity and paired gain intervals, plus worst-family
utility. The task-family sampling rule and any averaging weights must be frozen;
an unweighted mean over researcher-selected families is not a population
estimand.

### M4 — MAJOR: familywise uncertainty is not simultaneous at the scale-claim level

The gate applies separate one-sided 95% Clopper--Pearson upper bounds to four
families and requires each to pass, without a family-level error allocation.
The reported zero of 96 failures would likely survive a four-family
Bonferroni correction, but the present procedure does not establish that
simultaneous claim, and the gap grows rapidly with learned-task families.
Prefix-gain and latency gates are point estimates without uncertainty.

Mandatory control: allocate error across all frozen families or use a valid
hierarchical simultaneous procedure. Preserve per-repetition paired outputs so
gain, latency, and nonvacuity receive confidence intervals. No family or scale
may be selected after viewing these intervals.

### M5 — MAJOR: monotone nested Bernoulli loss is fragile under realistic action utility

The CSA loop stops at the first uncertified prefix and is justified only when
failure events are nested. “Any safety violation in the first `p` actions” is
nested; task loss, recovery, reward shortfall, and prefix-specific constraints
need not be. Learned rollouts can also map nominally identical prefixes to
different state distributions after an early model error.

Mandatory control: define one auditable binary nested event and verify nesting
row-by-row before certification. Report useful-task metrics separately. If the
learned loss is nonnested or continuous, use a theorem matching that loss;
sorting or cumulative-max repair after observing outcomes is not sufficient.

### m1 — MINOR: terminology should distinguish three different quantities

Use distinct names for (i) confidence miscoverage/false certification, (ii)
realized episode safety violation, and (iii) normalized prefix length. Calling
the third a “nonvacuity rate” and the first “true validity” invites deployment
misinterpretation.

### m2 — MINOR: scale curves reuse nested samples without covariance reporting

Nested samples are efficient and legitimate for a descriptive curve, but the
five points are highly correlated. Report paired trajectories or cluster-aware
intervals and do not fit or select a crossover scale from the same curve used
for confirmation.

## Mandatory learned-dynamics gate

Before execution, the learned-dynamics integration must freeze and publish:

1. **Estimand and ledger:** per-episode risk `alpha_t`, confidence spend
   `delta_t`, repeated-decision spending rule, loss event, task utility, and
   exact statistical unit.
2. **Family registry:** model checkpoints, policies, prefix generator, prefix
   grid, real exit schedule, thresholds, task/shift populations, subgroup
   weights, and fully held-out families.
3. **Four-way split:** model training, shift calibration, certification
   rollouts, and true execution evaluation with disjoint unit/cluster IDs.
4. **Exchangeability audit:** coupling mechanism, independent-pair control,
   temporal/off-policy/hard-state stress cells, effective sample size, and
   calibration expiry/recalibration triggers.
5. **Comparator parity:** actual realized exits; optimized union,
   anytime-CP/mixture, fixed-time, abstain, execute, and model-only controls;
   equal candidate family and confidence budget.
6. **Grid robustness:** multiple frozen risk budgets, near-boundary alternatives,
   prefix and exit coarsening/refinement, irrelevant-candidate insertion, and a
   composite-alternative e-process.
7. **Nonvacuity and utility:** nonabstention CI, prefix distribution, oracle-safe
   fraction, subgroup minima, realized violation CI, return/success, and
   rollback or recovery cost.
8. **End-to-end systems evidence:** p50/p95/p99 decision latency and throughput
   including rollout, calibration amortization, every exit update, inference,
   synchronization, selection, and dispatch; memory and utilization included.
9. **Simultaneous verdict:** family-adjusted true-environment validity and
   preregistered paired lower bounds for utility gain over the strongest robust
   baseline. Average point gains are insufficient.

## Required falsifiers

The learned-dynamics version must STOP a scale/deployment claim if any of the
following occurs:

- true-environment false certification exceeds the simultaneous tolerance in
  any required family or subgroup;
- calibration/evaluation exchangeability fails or the usable coupling produces
  a vacuous envelope;
- CSA advantage disappears under actual/coarsened exit schedules, family-grid
  perturbations, near-threshold risks, or an optimized union baseline;
- repeated episode spending is absent or realized violations exceed the locked
  budget;
- nonabstention, useful return, or oracle-relative prefix efficiency fails its
  locked lower bound;
- end-to-end latency/throughput advantage disappears after calibration,
  rollouts, synchronization, and action dispatch are counted.

Until these controls pass on fresh learned tasks and shifts, the only supported
claim is: P6.3's finite-grid robust CSA specialization was valid and positively
selective in its frozen IID nested-Bernoulli benchmark. It does not establish
learned-dynamics scale readiness.
